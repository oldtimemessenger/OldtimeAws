🏦 3. But bro, how do YOU get paid?

This is the important part.

Suppose users spend $10,000 on eligible in-app purchases.

The money generally flows like this:

Users → Apple/Google → Applicable fees and adjustments → Your developer proceeds

Apple and Google pay your developer account according to their respective payment schedules and agreements. Your actual proceeds depend on applicable fees, refunds, taxes, and other adjustments.

Then your own system handles creator earnings.

For example:

* Oldtimehive receives its proceeds from eligible purchases.
* Your backend records the amount attributable to each creator.
* You calculate each creator’s eligible share.
* You maintain their earnings balance.
* You arrange payouts through a suitable payout provider.

Apple and Google handle the original purchase. They do not automatically handle your entire creator payout operation.

You’ll need to consider payout verification, fraud prevention, tax reporting, and any legal requirements applicable to your business.

⚠️ 4. There’s one thing you need to understand about skipping Stripe

Bro, there are actually two different payment problems.

Problem 1: Collecting money from users.

You can use Apple In-App Purchase and Google Play Billing for eligible digital purchases instead of Stripe.

Problem 2: Paying creators their earnings.

You still need a way to transfer money to creators’ bank accounts or other supported payout destinations.

You might eventually use a provider specializing in marketplace payouts, even if you never use Stripe for checkout.

You don’t necessarily need to build that payout infrastructure today.

Also, since you’re developing Oldtimehive as a mobile-first web app, there’s an important distinction: Apple and Google billing aren’t automatically available to an ordinary website. You’ll need the appropriate native app integrations and store-compliant implementation when distributing through their app stores.

If you later distribute a web version, its payment options will need to be evaluated separately.

🔥 5. Here’s the architecture I’d choose for Oldtimehive

Since you’re building on AWS and want to move away from Replit, I’d organize the system like this:

Component	Responsibility
Oldtimehive frontend	Displays subscriptions, coin packages, gifts, and purchase status
Apple StoreKit	Handles eligible iPhone purchases
Google Play Billing	Handles eligible Android purchases
AWS backend	Verifies transactions and processes purchase events
PostgreSQL database	Stores balances, transactions, subscriptions, and creator earnings
Creator payout system	Eventually transfers eligible earnings to creators

The backend must be the authority for coin balances and creator earnings. Never trust the frontend alone to tell your server that someone paid.

For subscriptions, use store notifications and server-side verification to keep access synchronized when a user renews, cancels, or receives a refund.

One more thing about your web app

You don’t have to stop building features just because the payment system isn’t ready.

You can build the coin shop interface, creator subscription pages, gift animations, and earnings dashboard now. Keep actual purchases disabled or in test mode until the billing integrations are implemented.

That way, you won’t have to redesign your whole app later.
