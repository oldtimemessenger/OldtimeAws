BRO. THIS ONE IS A MUST. 😭

The effect library should include:

Cinematic Title	Creates a dramatic title composition around the subject

The feature that would make yours special

I would build a Depth Composer.

A creator could:

1. Take or upload a photo.
2. Tap the person in the image.
3. Let the app isolate the person from the background.
4. Add enormous text behind them.
5. Place another word in front of their shoulder.
6. Adjust the depth, blur, shadows and lighting.
7. Move everything with their fingers.

The editor would automatically create the correct layer order.

For example:

* Background: a city at night.
* Middle layer: giant text spelling OLD TIME.
* Foreground: the creator’s portrait.
* Top layer: a small title crossing in front of the creator’s shoulder.

The result would have genuine visual depth.

Important: Text doesn’t automatically understand what’s behind a person. The app needs a subject mask or segmentation model, plus a proper layer compositor. That is what makes this more sophisticated than merely placing text over a photograph.

CapCut itself documents a workflow for creating text-behind-person effects using duplicated footage and overlays.

These modes change how one layer combines with the pixels beneath it.

For example, Screen can help combine light effects with a dark background, while Multiply can darken a layer to integrate it into a composition.

Not every mode is appropriate for every image. Giving creators control is what makes the tool useful.

Edge refinement deserves special attention

Remember the remix feature you wanted, where the creator appears naturally integrated into the original content?

A rough cutout with jagged hair edges will immediately make it look cheap.

The system needs to preserve fine details around hair, shoulders and clothing, then composite the person with the correct background, lighting and shadows.

I’d treat edge quality as a core capability rather than a cosmetic upgrade.

🔥 10. What would make Oldtimehive better than the competition?

Here is where I’d focus the product strategy.

First: intelligent depth effects.

A creator types a title, taps a person, and the app intelligently places the text behind them. No complicated manual masking for the basic result.

Second: professional presets that remain editable.

A cover shouldn’t become a flattened image the moment someone applies a template. The user should be able to change every title, layer, color and effect afterward.

Third: combinations, not isolated filters.

A single preset could combine typography, shadows, lighting, texture and background treatments into a cohesive design.

Fourth: one project for multiple formats.

Create a cover, then adapt it for a Story, a vertical video thumbnail and a profile promotion without rebuilding everything.

Fifth: reusable creator styles.

Let users save their favorite fonts, effects, colors, spacing and cover layouts as their personal style.

Sixth: smart assistance without taking away control.

The app could recommend a composition based on the photo, but creators should always be able to move, resize, recolor and rearrange its elements.

Seventh: high-quality export.

Preserve image sharpness, support suitable output resolutions and prevent text from becoming blurry when a composition is exported.

That’s how you build a studio rather than a collection of filters.

You do not need to develop every effect from scratch before launching.

I’d build the foundation first:

1. A proper layer-based canvas.
2. Free-position text and image objects.
3. Typography presets and editable effects.
4. Background and texture treatments.
5. Subject cutouts and masking.
6. The Depth Composer for text behind people.
7. Advanced compositing and professional cover templates.

Then expand the library as the core engine becomes reliable.

One technical detail matters here: because Oldtimehive is currently a mobile-first web app, we need to test these effects on actual iPhones and Android devices. Complex masking, 3D typography and high-resolution compositing can be demanding, and a browser editor must be engineered carefully to stay responsive.

My recommendation

Call the feature Oldtimehive Studio, with a standard editor for quick creations and a Pro Studio for advanced work.

The aim isn’t simply to give users more effects. It’s to make the effects work together so that a beginner can produce something that looks professionally designed.

And the first feature I’d perfect is text that goes behind a person, in front of a person, and around the subject in the same composition.

That one feature, when implemented well, can make an ordinary portrait look dramatically more sophisticated.
