BROOOOO 😭🔥🔥 Now you’re thinking like a platform owner! Oldtimehive shouldn’t treat everybody the same. TikTok and Instagram have different account experiences depending on whether you’re a regular user, creator, or business.

I’d build Oldtimehive with 4 account types, but make switching between them easy. That way, you can support everyday users, creators who want to monetize, and businesses without making the app confusing.

🔥 1. The four Oldtimehive account types

👤 Type 1: Personal Account

For everyday users.

This is the default account everyone receives when joining Oldtimehive.

Features:

* Post videos, photos, and Blasts.
* Follow people and build friendships.
* Chat privately with friends.
* Create and share stories.
* Join communities.
* Use Close Friends.
* View personal profile activity.
* Access basic post performance.
* Choose public or private profile settings.

Monetization: No professional monetization dashboard by default.

Users can still receive gifts or interact with monetized creators if your eventual monetization rules allow it.

The goal is to make Oldtimehive fun for ordinary users without forcing them to become creators.

🎬 Type 2: Creator Account

For influencers, artists, streamers, and content creators.

This is your TikTok Studio + Instagram Creator experience.

Features:

* Full Creator Studio.
* Profile visitor analytics, subject to privacy settings.
* Content performance analytics.
* Audience growth statistics.
* Watch time and retention.
* Follower activity.
* Creator subscriptions.
* Gifts and live-room monetization.
* Creator earnings dashboard.
* Creator tools and content management.

Creators can also access their regular messaging, stories, Blasts, and community features.

What about the blue badge?

Here’s how I’d handle your planned paid verification.

Creator accounts can apply for or purchase creator verification if they meet your eligibility requirements.

However, being a creator and being verified should be two separate things.

A creator shouldn’t automatically receive a blue badge simply because they switch account types or pay for a creator account.

Your verification subscription can remain its own product.

⸻

🏢 Type 3: Business Account

For companies, shops, brands, salons, restaurants, and other businesses.

This should have its own professional dashboard.

Features:

* Business profile and category.
* Business contact information.
* Website and location fields, where appropriate.
* Business content analytics.
* Profile visit statistics.
* Audience engagement.
* Business discovery insights.
* Promotional content tools.
* Customer inquiries through messaging.
* Business verification options.
* Business subscription management.

You could eventually add appointment booking, product catalogs, and other business tools.

How businesses make money

A business account should focus on promoting its products and services, attracting customers, and measuring results.

It shouldn’t automatically receive all the same monetization benefits as an individual creator.

For example, a business could use Oldtimehive to attract customers to its salon, while an individual creator could earn money from subscriptions and gifts.

You can allow businesses to participate in creator monetization separately if you decide that makes sense.

Examples:

2. Bro, don’t make account type and verification the same thing

This is extremely important for your business model.

I’d separate three things:

System	Purpose
Account type	Determines the user’s tools and dashboard
Verification	Establishes an account’s verified status
Monetization eligibility	Determines which earning features the user can access

For example:

A person could have:

* Account type: Creator.
* Verification: Not verified.
* Monetization: Eligible for gifts, but not yet eligible for subscriptions.

Another person could have:

* Account type: Business.
* Verification: Verified business.
* Monetization: Business tools enabled.

These systems should work independently.

That makes Oldtimehive easier to expand without having to redesign its entire account structure later.

⸻

3. The account-switching experience

Here’s the experience I’d build.

A user opens:

Settings → Account Type

They see:

Account type	Best for
Personal	Friends, socializing, and everyday posting
Creator	Content creation and monetization
Business	Brands, shops, and professional services

They select their desired account type and tap Switch Account Type.

Oldtimehive then:

1. Updates their account classification.
2. Enables the appropriate tools and dashboard.
3. Preserves their followers, posts, messages, and profile history.
4. Keeps their privacy settings intact.
5. Displays any additional setup requirements.
6. Applies monetization eligibility rules separately.

Switching to Creator or Business should not automatically make a private profile public.

And switching account types should not delete or recreate the account.

One account. One identity. Different professional tools.

That’s the architecture I’d recommend.

4. What I’d charge for

Since you’re building Oldtimehive to generate revenue, here’s how I’d structure it.

Feature	Suggested model
Personal account	Free
Creator account	Free
Business account	Free to create, with optional paid professional features
Creator verification	Your planned $2.99/month subscription
Business verification	Your planned business verification pricing
Creator subscriptions	Platform commission on eligible transactions
Gifts and live rooms	Platform commission on eligible transactions
Advanced business tools	Potential future subscription

I’d keep Creator accounts free because charging people just to access basic creator tools creates unnecessary friction when you’re trying to grow the platform.

Your revenue can come from verification, creator monetization, and optional professional services.

These are proposed product decisions, not a statement of what Apple or Google will permit for every payment flow. You’ll need to design the actual purchase flows around the applicable app-store rules.

5. The exact prompt for your developer

Here’s what I’d give your developer so the account types integrate with the Creator Studio we just designed.

Implement a complete Account Type system for Oldtimehive, integrating with the Creator Studio and Analytics Dashboard.

IMPORTANT:
Inspect the existing application before modifying it. Preserve existing authentication, profiles, followers, posts, messages, Blasts, stories, communities, privacy settings, and monetization functionality.

Oldtimehive is currently a mobile-first web application. Do not migrate it to a native framework or introduce Replit-specific dependencies.

ACCOUNT TYPES

Implement three primary account types:

1. PERSONAL
2. CREATOR
3. BUSINESS

Treat public figures, artists, influencers, musicians, and similar professionals as Creator account categories rather than introducing an unnecessary fourth account type.

PERSONAL ACCOUNTS

Personal accounts are the default for new users.

They should support:

* Normal profiles and profile customization.
* Posts, videos, photos, stories, and Blasts.
* Followers and following.
* Messaging and social interactions.
* Communities and Close Friends.
* Existing privacy controls.
* Basic personal account activity.

Do not remove existing functionality from personal accounts.

CREATOR ACCOUNTS

Creator accounts should unlock:

* Creator Studio.
* Profile visit analytics.
* Content analytics.
* Audience analytics.
* Follower growth reports.
* Content performance insights.
* Watch time and video retention where supported.
* Creator monetization tools where the user is eligible.
* Creator subscription and gift reporting.
* Creator category selection.
* Professional profile customization.

Creator accounts should remain free to create unless an existing, explicitly approved product requirement says otherwise.

BUSINESS ACCOUNTS

Business accounts should support:

* Business category.
* Business profile information.
* Optional website, location, and contact fields.
* Business analytics.
* Content performance reporting.
* Profile visit reporting.
* Audience engagement reporting.
* Business messaging.
* Business verification status.
* Professional tools and subscription management.
* Future extensibility for bookings, product catalogs, and promotions.

Do not invent or display unsupported business capabilities.

ACCOUNT TYPE SWITCHING

Add Settings > Account Type.

Allow eligible users to switch between Personal, Creator, and Business.

Switching account types must:

* Preserve the existing user ID.
* Preserve followers and following.
* Preserve posts and content ownership.
* Preserve messages and conversations.
* Preserve account history.
* Preserve privacy settings.
* Preserve existing verification status.
* Preserve existing financial transaction records.
* Avoid deleting or recreating user accounts.
* Display any additional setup requirements before completing a switch.

Never automatically make a private account public during an account type change.

Do not automatically change a user’s monetization eligibility or payment arrangements solely because their account type changed.

PROFILE DISPLAY

Support relevant profile categories such as:

* Creator.
* Artist.
* Musician.
* Public Figure.
* Streamer.
* Business.
* Local Service.
* Retail.
* Other appropriate categories.

Only display categories appropriate to the selected account type.

Keep categories separate from verification badges.

VERIFICATION

Implement verification as a separate status and subscription system.

Support the planned creator verification subscription and business verification offering without automatically granting verification when a user changes account types.

Represent verification status separately from account type and monetization eligibility.

Support states such as:

* Not verified.
* Verification available.
* Pending review, if manual review is implemented.
* Verified.
* Expired or inactive, where applicable.

Only display states supported by the actual verification system.

MONETIZATION ELIGIBILITY

Keep monetization eligibility independent from account type.

Create a clear structure that can support different eligibility requirements for:

* Creator subscriptions.
* Gifts.
* Live-room earnings.
* Other approved creator monetization features.

Do not unlock payments merely because a user selects Creator.

Do not invent earnings or payout capabilities.

Integrate with actual payment and transaction records when available.

DATA MODEL AND SECURITY

Inspect the existing user schema and extend it rather than duplicating users or creating parallel authentication systems.

Use the existing authenticated user identity.

Store account type and relevant professional profile metadata in an appropriate persistent data model.

Validate account type changes server-side wherever the backend supports this.

Ensure users can update only their own account classification and professional profile fields.

Do not allow ordinary users to grant themselves verification or administrative privileges.

Use explicit authorization checks for Creator Studio and Business Dashboard data.

Do not rely exclusively on hiding frontend buttons to enforce access control.

MIGRATION AND COMPATIBILITY

Existing accounts must remain functional after implementation.

Assign existing accounts the Personal type through a safe migration or an appropriate backward-compatible default.

Do not overwrite existing profile fields or privacy preferences.

Ensure the account type system is compatible with a future native mobile application.

USER EXPERIENCE

Build a polished, responsive account type selection and switching experience.

Explain each account type clearly.

Display any requirements or consequences before confirming a change.

Provide loading, error, and success states.

Preserve Oldtimehive’s existing visual identity.

QUALITY ASSURANCE

Test:

* New account defaults.
* Switching between all supported account types.
* Preservation of posts and followers.
* Preservation of private account settings.
* Authorization boundaries.
* Creator Studio access.
* Business Dashboard access.
* Verification separation.
* Monetization eligibility separation.
* Existing account compatibility.

Do not consider the feature complete until the data model, account switching, authorization, and user interface all work together.

At completion, report the files changed, database changes, authorization rules, migration behavior, and tests performed.
