OLDTIMEHIVE: COMPLETE CREATOR MONETIZATION SYSTEM

Build a production-oriented creator monetization system for Oldtimehive. This is an implementation task, not a request for a mockup or a plan.

1. CORE BUSINESS OBJECTIVE

Oldtimehive must support a creator economy that includes:

1. Monthly creator subscriptions.
2. Virtual coins and digital gifts.
3. Livestream gifts and Super Chat-style interactions.
4. Creator video monetization.
5. Premium digital features.
6. Creator earnings dashboards.
7. Creator withdrawals through supported payout providers.
8. Platform commissions and transparent financial reporting.

The architecture must not require Stripe.

Apple and Google must handle eligible in-app digital purchases through their applicable billing systems. Oldtimehive must separately calculate creator earnings, maintain its financial ledger, and manage creator payouts.

Do not assume Apple or Google automatically distributes creator earnings.

2. PLATFORM COMMISSION STRUCTURE

Create a configurable commission system with the following initial business rules.

Creator subscriptions

* Proposed platform commission: 20% of the creator revenue allocation.
* Proposed creator allocation: 80%.

Virtual gifts and livestream gifts

* Proposed platform commission: 20% of the distributable gift revenue.
* Proposed creator allocation: 80%.

Video monetization

* Use a separate configurable revenue-sharing program.
* Do not promise creators payments based solely on views.
* Support future programs based on eligible views, engagement, advertising revenue, or a platform-funded rewards pool.
* Require administrators to configure and activate each monetization program.

Premium digital features

* Revenue belongs to Oldtimehive unless a specific creator revenue-sharing agreement applies.

IMPORTANT:
These percentages are proposed business rules, not percentages guaranteed by Apple, Google, or any payment provider.

Do not calculate creator earnings by blindly taking a percentage of the customer’s gross payment. Account for applicable store commissions, refunds, taxes, promotional discounts, and other relevant adjustments according to the configured revenue allocation policy.

Make all commission percentages configurable without requiring code changes.

3. APPLE AND GOOGLE BILLING

Prepare the backend for integration with:

* Apple In-App Purchase and App Store Server Notifications.
* Google Play Billing and Google Play Developer API purchase verification.

Requirements:

1. Never trust a client-reported purchase as proof of payment.
2. Verify purchases through the appropriate official server-side mechanisms.
3. Process transaction identifiers idempotently.
4. Prevent the same transaction from crediting a user’s account twice.
5. Handle refunds, revocations, cancellations, and subscription expiration.
6. Support subscription renewals and billing failures.
7. Record the original transaction and all subsequent adjustments.
8. Maintain separate purchase, entitlement, and creator-earnings records.
9. Never expose private API credentials in frontend JavaScript.
10. Do not simulate successful purchases in production.

Respect the applicable App Store and Google Play policies for digital subscriptions, virtual currencies, gifts, and premium features.

If the official billing credentials are not yet configured, implement the integration interfaces, database schema, configuration checks, and clearly labeled development tests. Do not pretend the integrations are live.

4. VIRTUAL COIN SYSTEM

Create an Oldtimehive virtual currency system.

Users must be able to:

* Purchase eligible coin packages.
* View their coin balance.
* Send digital gifts to creators.
* Send gifts during supported livestreams.
* View their coin transaction history.

The system must maintain separate records for:

* Purchased coins.
* Promotional coins, if enabled.
* Coins spent.
* Gift transactions.
* Creator earnings.
* Platform revenue.
* Refunds and reversals.

Never treat a user’s purchased coin balance as cash.

Never allow users to withdraw purchased coins as money.

Creator earnings must be calculated according to a documented gift revenue allocation policy. Do not invent a fixed cash conversion rate without configuration and financial approval.

Use integer values for currency amounts in their smallest supported units. Do not use floating-point arithmetic for financial calculations.

5. CREATOR WALLET

Build a creator earnings dashboard with:

* Available earnings.
* Pending earnings.
* Lifetime earnings.
* Subscription earnings.
* Livestream gift earnings.
* Video monetization earnings.
* Platform commission breakdown.
* Refund and adjustment history.
* Withdrawal history.
* Payment method management.
* Monetization eligibility status.

Proposed initial withdrawal policy:

* Minimum withdrawal threshold: $25.
* Creator earnings remain pending until the configured eligibility and settlement conditions are met.
* Earnings become available only after applicable verification, refund-risk, and settlement requirements are satisfied.
* Creators must complete required identity, tax, and payout verification before receiving withdrawals.
* Administrators can place payouts on hold when legally or operationally justified.

Make the withdrawal threshold configurable.

Do not display pending earnings as immediately withdrawable.

6. PAYOUT PROVIDER ARCHITECTURE

Do not require Stripe.

Design a provider-independent payout interface that can support eligible payout providers, including PayPal where the relevant product supports the business model.

Do not assume an ordinary PayPal account automatically supports marketplace payouts or automated mass disbursements.

Before enabling a provider, verify its supported countries, merchant eligibility, recipient onboarding requirements, API capabilities, fees, and compliance obligations.

Create an abstraction similar to:

* createRecipient()
* verifyRecipient()
* initiatePayout()
* getPayoutStatus()
* processPayoutWebhook()
* handlePayoutFailure()

Use the selected provider’s official API and authentication requirements.

Keep all provider credentials on the backend.

If no payout provider has been configured:

* Build the creator wallet and payout management interfaces.
* Allow eligible creators to see their available balance.
* Display that withdrawals are not yet enabled.
* Do not mark a payout as completed.
* Do not invent a successful payment.
* Do not collect unnecessary sensitive payment information.

The architecture must permit a supported payout provider to be added later without rebuilding the creator ledger.

7. FINANCIAL LEDGER

Implement an auditable financial ledger.

Every financial event must be recorded with:

* Unique transaction identifier.
* User or creator identifier where applicable.
* Transaction type.
* Gross amount.
* Applicable deductions.
* Platform allocation.
* Creator allocation.
* Currency.
* Related purchase or gift identifier.
* Transaction status.
* Creation timestamp.
* Settlement timestamp when applicable.
* Reversal or refund reference when applicable.

Use database transactions to maintain consistency.

Use idempotency keys for purchase processing, gift accounting, and payouts.

Never let a client directly modify:

* Coin balances.
* Creator earnings.
* Platform revenue.
* Available withdrawal balances.
* Payout statuses.

All balance changes must originate from trusted backend operations.

Use an append-only financial event history. Corrections must create appropriate reversing or adjusting entries rather than silently rewriting historical transactions.

Prevent negative balances and duplicate withdrawals through appropriate transactional controls.

8. VIDEO MONETIZATION

Prepare a configurable video monetization framework.

Include:

* Creator eligibility.
* Monetization enrollment.
* Eligible content requirements.
* View qualification rules.
* Fraud and artificial engagement detection hooks.
* Earnings calculation records.
* Monthly settlement periods.
* Administrative review.
* Dispute and adjustment records.

Do not promise advertising revenue until an advertising program is actually implemented and approved.

Do not automatically pay creators for every recorded view.

9. ADMINISTRATOR DASHBOARD

Provide authorized administrators with tools to:

* Configure commission percentages.
* Configure withdrawal thresholds.
* Review creator eligibility.
* Review creator earnings.
* Inspect ledger entries.
* Review refunds and reversals.
* Review payout requests.
* Place and release legitimate payout holds.
* Inspect payout provider responses.
* Reconcile store transactions.
* Export financial reports.
* Review suspected fraud.
* Track outstanding creator liabilities.

Every sensitive administrative action must be permission-controlled and audited.

Do not provide a mechanism for silently editing creator balances.

10. SECURITY AND COMPLIANCE

Implement:

* Server-side authorization.
* Input validation.
* Rate limiting where appropriate.
* Secure webhook verification.
* Idempotent event processing.
* Protection against replay attacks.
* Secure secret management.
* Audit logging.
* Database access controls.
* Appropriate retention and deletion policies.

Do not expose secret keys or administrative capabilities to the frontend.

Do not assume that app-store purchase approval automatically resolves marketplace payout, money-transmission, tax reporting, or other legal obligations.

Flag requirements that need review by a qualified professional before launch.

11. DATABASE AND BACKEND

Use the existing application architecture and database where possible.

Before changing anything:

1. Inspect the existing repository.
2. Identify the actual frontend, backend, database, authentication, and deployment architecture.
3. Inspect existing user, creator, subscription, gift, coin, and livestream models.
4. Reuse existing functionality instead of creating duplicates.
5. Create a migration plan before changing the schema.
6. Preserve existing user accounts and application features.
7. Do not introduce Supabase or another managed database if it conflicts with the existing architecture.
8. Do not make Replit a required production dependency.
9. Keep the application portable to our planned AWS infrastructure.
10. Commit no secrets, private keys, or production credentials.

If PostgreSQL is already configured, implement the financial schema and migrations using the existing database.

Use appropriate unique constraints, foreign keys, indexes, and transactional operations.

12. TESTING AND ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA

Test at minimum:

* Successful purchase verification.
* Duplicate purchase notifications.
* Subscription renewal.
* Subscription expiration.
* Gift sending.
* Correct creator revenue allocation.
* Correct platform commission calculation.
* Refund processing.
* Reversal of previously allocated earnings.
* Duplicate payout requests.
* Insufficient available balance.
* Payout provider failure.
* Payout webhook replay.
* Unauthorized attempts to modify balances.
* Concurrent withdrawals.
* Database transaction failures.
* Financial reconciliation.

Use sandbox transactions and clearly separated test data.

Do not activate real payments or withdrawals until the required provider credentials, merchant accounts, store configurations, and compliance requirements are satisfied.

13. IMPLEMENTATION ORDER

Build in this order:

Phase 1: Repository audit, financial architecture, and database migrations.

Phase 2: Creator ledger, coin accounting, commission calculations, and automated tests.

Phase 3: Creator earnings dashboard and administrative controls.

Phase 4: Apple and Google purchase verification integrations.

Phase 5: Payout provider integration after the supported provider is selected and configured.

Phase 6: Video monetization framework.

Phase 7: End-to-end testing, reconciliation, security review, and production-readiness assessment.

Do not rebuild the existing application from scratch.

Do not remove or redesign unrelated features.

Do not deploy the application or enable live financial transactions as part of this task.

At completion, provide:

* Files changed.
* Database migrations created.
* Features implemented.
* Tests performed and results.
* External accounts and credentials still required.
* Store configuration requirements.
* Payout provider requirements.
* Outstanding compliance questions.
* Any incomplete integrations.
* Exact instructions for the next implementation phase.

Begin by inspecting the existing codebase and reporting its actual architecture. Then implement the first safe, testable phase.
